Simulator Controls & Features

Navigation & Editing

  • Pan/Zoom: Click and drag empty space to pan. Scroll mouse wheel to zoom.
  • Adjust Volume: Click a node (or row) to select it, then scroll your mouse wheel to increase/decrease its volume.
  • Multi-Select: Hold CTRL or CMD while clicking to select and drag multiple nodes at once.

M&A and Remedies

  • Mergers: Click the chain icon () on a company card, then click another company's chain icon to merge them.
  • Divestitures (Remedies): Grab the drag handle (⋮⋮) on a site row and drop it onto a competitor's card to transfer ownership.

Toolbar

Use the bottom toolbar to add/remove companies, change the warning threshold, change the catchment size (Dmax), or instantly generate a new random market scenario.

The Math of Local Overlap Analysis

In competition economics, authorities (like the FTC or CMA) evaluate retail mergers by analyzing specific geographic catchments. If a merger creates a local monopoly, it harms consumers in that specific town.

1. The Catchment Area (Isochrone)

We define a local market by a Maximum Distance (Dmax), representing how far a consumer is willing to travel (e.g., a 15-minute drive).

2. Distance Weighting

Competitors located right next door exert massive pressure, while competitors on the edge of the catchment exert very little. We weight each store's volume by its distance ($d$) using linear decay:

Weight = (Dmax - d) / Dmax

3. Calculating Market Share

To find a specific site's local market share, we take its volume, add the weighted volume of all allied stores in range, and divide it by the total weighted volume of ALL stores in range.

Share = Σ(Allied Weighted Vol) / Σ(Total Weighted Vol)

If this share exceeds the regulatory "Safe Harbor" threshold (e.g., 35%), the authorities will likely demand a divestiture remedy.
